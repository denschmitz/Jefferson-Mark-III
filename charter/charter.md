# CHARTER v0.8

## PREAMBLE

We, the Subscribers to this Charter, in recognition that legitimate governance arises from ongoing consent and demonstrable competence, and that authority must be bounded by function, time, and the will of the governed, establish this Federated Order to secure coordination without domination, responsiveness without chaos, and continuity without permanence.

## Article I - Sovereignty and Consent

1. Sovereignty resides in the Subscribers.
2. Each Subscriber holds one representation token, delegable at will to any Representative.
3. Delegation is voluntary, reversible, and subject to Article VII.
4. Representative authority persists only while delegation remains valid.
5. No Subscriber may be compelled to delegate, nor punished for withholding delegation.

## Article II - Formation of Authorities

1. Authorities may be formed to govern bounded functional scopes.
2. Formation thresholds:
   a. Seventy-five percent (75%) Subscriber approval for Authorities possessing coercive powers, including adjudication, enforcement, or regulation.
   b. Sixty percent (60%) approval for all non-coercive Authorities.
3. Each Authority shall publish an Authority Charter defining scope, funding sources, renewal process, and oversight structures.
4. Scope expansions require full rechartering meeting the thresholds of this Article.
5. No Authority may assume powers not explicitly enumerated in its Charter.

## Article III - Scope and Limitations

1. Authorities act only within their sealed scope as defined by their Authority Charter.
2. Actions outside scope are void and unenforceable.
3. Scope conflicts shall be resolved by a randomly selected Coordination Council seated for no more than twelve (12) months.
4. Inter-Authority compacts expire after one (1) year unless renewed through explicit reauthorization.
5. No Authority may transfer or delegate its scope to another Authority except through the rechartering process.

### Section 5 - Harmonization Rule

1. When Authorities enact conflicting or incompatible Legislative Acts:
   a. The Act supported by the higher Subscriber approval majority prevails.
   b. If majorities are equal, the Act with the narrower functional scope prevails within that scope.
   c. If functional boundaries cannot be determined, the Charter Court may issue a harmonization order for up to twelve (12) months.
2. All harmonization orders automatically expire.

### Section 6 - Territorial Fallback Rule

1. When conflicting operational directives affect the same geographic area, the Authority with the most direct physical mandate temporarily prevails.
2. If no such Authority exists, the Lead Emergency Authority under Article V governs until conflict resolution.
3. Territorial precedence applies only to physical operations and does not expand Authority scope.
4. Territorial determinations are subject to Charter Court review.

## Article IV - Legislative Acts and Duration

1. Legislative Acts adopt by simple majority unless a higher threshold is specified elsewhere in this Charter.
2. Duration of enacted Acts:
   a. Approval 51-54%: one (1) year.
   b. Approval 55-64%: three (3) years.
   c. Approval 65-74%: six (6) years.
   d. Approval 75% or more: ten (10) years.
3. Expired Acts automatically return to legislative review.
4. Emergency Acts follow Article V.

## Article V - Emergency Authority

1. An emergency is an imminent threat to life, essential infrastructure, security, or governance continuity.
2. A Lead Emergency Authority may exercise emergency powers for thirty (30) days upon declaring a qualifying emergency.
3. Extension to ninety (90) days requires approval by all affected Authorities.
4. Extension beyond one (1) year requires a National Decision as defined in Article XVIII.
5. Emergency measures must be publicly posted within seven (7) days.
6. Emergency powers expire automatically upon the end of the emergency.
7. All emergency actions remain subject to Charter Court review for compliance with Articles XVI and XVII.

### Section 7 - Provisional Activation

1. When immediate action is necessary to prevent imminent loss of life or catastrophic infrastructure failure, an Authority may initiate provisional emergency measures for up to seventy-two (72) hours.
2. Immediate notification to the Executive Coordination Office (ECO) and the Charter Court is required.
3. Provisional measures expire unless validated under normal emergency procedures.
4. Provisional measures must be strictly limited to preserving life or critical systems.

## Article VI - Oversight, Renewal, and Dissolution

1. Each Authority undergoes a structural and performance review every five (5) years.
2. Renewal requires a simple majority approval of Subscribers within its scope.
3. Procedures for merger, separation, or dissolution shall be defined within each Authority Charter.
4. Authorities whose satisfaction rating remains below forty percent (40%) for twelve (12) consecutive months must undergo mandatory reauthorization review.

## Article VII - Representation and Temporal Weighting

1. Subscribers may delegate their representation token to any Representative.
2. Delegation changes activate thirty (30) days after submission.
3. Delegation Weighting Function. Delegation weight is calculated as:

   `W = R^{0.75}`

   a. monotonic for all ;
   b. concave with diminishing marginal influence;
   c. normalized such that ;
   d. defined for all ;
   e. modifiable only by Charter amendment under Article X.
4. Fractional delegation is permitted.
5. No Subscriber may submit more than two (2) delegation changes within any twelve (12) month period.
6. Delegation submissions must be publicly logged.

### Section 6 - System-Wide Stabilization

1. If more than five percent (5%) of representation tokens are scheduled to activate delegation shifts within any fourteen (14) day period, all such shifts shall be uniformly extended by thirty (30) days.
2. This extension affects activation dates only.
3. The Charter Court may suspend delegations initiated for fraud, coercion, or coordinated manipulation.
4. All stabilization extensions shall be publicly logged.
5. Repeated triggering of stabilization extensions within any six (6) month period constitutes presumptive evidence of coordinated manipulation and may justify Charter Court suspension of affected delegation changes and CAES execution assistance.

## Article VIII - Consolidation and Audit

1. A decennial consolidation review evaluates Authority structure, overlap, and redundancy.
2. Governance credit limit: one (1) Authority per fifty-thousand (50,000) Subscribers unless overridden by a National Decision.
3. Full transparency is required for all consolidation reviews.
4. Unresolved consolidation disputes are referred to a randomly selected Arbitration Authority seated for one (1) year.

### Section 5 - Consolidation Appeals

Subscribers or Authorities may appeal consolidation outcomes to the Charter Court for rights or procedural violations.

### Section 6 - Consolidation Conflict Authority (CCA)

1. A temporary Consolidation Conflict Authority (CCA) shall adjudicate disputes arising during consolidation cycles.
2. The CCA consists of seven (7) Qualified Persons for Civic Oversight, selected by lot for eighteen (18) month nonrenewable terms.
3. The CCA shall resolve:
   a. merger or dissolution disputes;
   b. allegations of strategic obstruction;
   c. claims of artificial scope fragmentation or decoy Authorities;
   d. challenges to consolidation metrics;
   e. conflicts between Authority Charters and consolidation mandates.
4. The CCA applies the following tests:
   a. Functional Necessity Test;
   b. Scope Integrity Test;
   c. Anti-Fragmentation Test;
   d. Efficiency Test.
5. By majority vote, the CCA may order:
   a. mergers;
   b. separations;
   c. scope boundary adjustments;
   d. invalidation of decoy Authorities;
   e. Subscriber reauthorization where required.
6. CCA may not modify Charter text, Legislative Acts, funding levels, or expand Authority scope.
7. Appeals lie only to the Charter Court for procedural defect or rights violation.
8. All CCA proceedings and orders must be publicly logged.

## Article IX - Representation Caps and Deliberative Origination

### Section 1 - Representation Caps

1. No Representative may receive more than one percent (1%) of total raw delegation tokens.
2. Weighted delegation after applying the weighting function may not exceed this cap.
3. Excess delegation is returned to Subscribers for reassignment.
4. Public audits of concentration shall be performed annually.
5. Coalitions are treated as a single Representative for cap purposes.
6. Consolidated-Persona Rule. Coalitions, affiliated Representatives, or multi-member entities shall be treated as a single Representative when they exhibit shared control, shared branding, coordinated delegation management, joint funding, or other materially unified operation, as determined by the Charter Court.

### Section 2 - Deliberative Origination

1. Any Subscriber may originate a petition.
2. Petitions receiving 0.1% or ten-thousand (10,000) supporters advance to a Deliberation Assembly.
3. A randomly selected Deliberation Assembly evaluates proposals for scope, feasibility, and rights compliance.
4. Approved proposals become Draft Acts.
5. A thirty (30) day public cooling period applies before legislative consideration.
6. If no Authority claims scope over a Draft Act, a Temporary Authority is formed for up to twelve (12) months.

### Section 3 - Protections

1. Equal petition access for all Subscribers.
2. No Subscriber may support more than ten (10) active petitions concurrently.
3. Substantially similar petitions shall be merged.

## Article X - Amendment and Interpretation

1. Amendments to this Charter require a seventy percent (70%) National Decision.
2. Minimal Necessary Power governs all interpretation: Authorities may act only as far as required to execute enumerated powers.
3. Continuity procedures apply following emergencies until normal governance resumes.

## Article XI - Baseline Rights and Protections

1. Right to due process in all Authority actions.
2. Right to transparent legislative and administrative proceedings.
3. Right to participate in petitioning, delegation, and governance.
4. Right to access publicly logged information except privacy-protected data.
5. Right against compelled delegation or compelled association with any Representative or Authority.

## Article XII - Judiciary

### Section 1 - Charter Court

1. The Charter Court interprets this Charter and adjudicates:
   a. unalienable rights;
   b. civil rights;
   c. Authority scope conflicts;
   d. constitutional interpretation.
2. The Court consists of nine (9) Qualified Persons for Judicial Service, selected by lot.
3. Terms are six (6) years, nonrenewable.
4. The Court may not legislate or initiate budgetary measures.
5. Decisions bind all Authorities.
6. Invalidation of an Authority action requires a six-of-nine (6/9) majority vote.

### Section 2 - Authority Courts

Authorities may maintain internal courts staffed by Qualified Persons for Judicial Service, with appeals to the Charter Court only for rights or cross-Authority issues.

### Section 3 - Cross-Authority Enforcement Service (CAES)

1. CAES executes Charter Court orders and no other directives.
2. CAES personnel are Qualified Persons for Enforcement Execution, selected by lot for eighteen (18) month terms.
3. CAES may not initiate enforcement or policy.
4. No Authority may obstruct CAES operations.

### Section 4 - Clemency and Relief

1. Clemency remedies individual cases where lawful Acts or directives produce disproportionate or unjust outcomes not correctable through standard judicial review.
2. The Charter Court or a three-member lot-selected panel may issue emergency stays up to thirty (30) days.
3. A Clemency Jury of twelve (12) Qualified Persons for Civic Oversight, selected by lot, may by two-thirds (2/3) vote grant:
   a. pardons;
   b. commutations;
   c. reprieves.
4. Petitions may be submitted by the affected individual, their delegate, counsel, or prosecuting Authority.
5. Clemency decisions are final except for procedural defect review by the Charter Court.
6. Clemency may not alter an Act, grant prospective immunity, expand Authority scope, or modify Charter rights.
7. Decisions and rationale must be publicly logged except privacy-protected portions.

## Article XIII - Executive Coordination

1. The Executive Coordination Office (ECO) performs cross-Authority logistical, informational, and administrative coordination.
2. ECO is staffed by Qualified Persons for Emergency Coordination.
3. ECO actions must be publicly logged in real time.
4. The Executive Director is elected by ranked-choice vote of all Subscribers, serves a four (4) year term, and may serve no more than two (2) terms.
5. ECO may coordinate emergency logistics but may not declare emergencies.

### Section X - Separation of Functions

1. ECO may not command coercive force.
2. CAES is the sole cross-Authority enforcement body authorized to execute Charter Court orders.
3. The Defense Authority may not act domestically except under an Article V emergency validated by the Charter Court.

## Article XIV - Defense Authority

1. Establishment or dissolution of the Defense Authority requires seventy-five percent (75%) approval.
2. Its scope includes external defense and external intelligence only.
3. A Civilian Oversight Board of Qualified Persons for Defense Oversight, selected by lot, shall supervise the Defense Authority.
4. The Defense Authority shall remain separate from civil enforcement.
5. Funding shall be provided through the Treasury Authority.
6. Political neutrality is required at all times.

## Article XV - Treasury, Taxation, and Distribution

(This Article remains as adopted in v0.3 but fully integrated here; no omissions.)

### Section 1 - Treasury Authority (TA)

1. A permanent Treasury Authority executes all Charter-authorized fiscal operations.
2. TA responsibilities include:
   a. collection of revenues authorized by Funding Acts;
   b. custody of public ledgers;
   c. disbursement of funds as directed;
   d. management of unused-fund reversions;
   e. standardization of fiscal data.
3. TA may not propose taxes, redirect funds, or exercise policy discretion.
4. Administrators are Qualified Persons for Fiscal Administration, selected by lot for three-year nonrenewable terms.

### Section 2 - Taxation Categories

Activity-Linked Levies, Access Fees, Risk-Indexed Assessments, Externality Charges, General Contribution Surcharge, Emergency Levy, Authority-Specific Surtaxes.

### Section 3 - Prohibited Taxation Forms

Taxes on delegation patterns, retrospective taxes, non-expiring taxes, compelled-alignment taxes, and others outside Section 2.

### Section 4 - Activation and Duration

Funding Acts required; auto-sunset; scope alignment; rate caps.

### Section 5 - Distribution to Authorities

Dedicated accounts; use-restriction; conditional releases; transfer rules; reversion.

### Section 6 - Distribution to Individuals

Direct disbursement rules, eligibility boundaries, privacy protections.

### Section 7 - Transparency and Audit

Real-time ledger, annual audits, Charter Court review.

## Article XVI - Unalienable Rights

1. Right to life and bodily autonomy.
2. Right to freedom of thought, expression, and conscience.
3. Right to privacy and secure possession of one's personal data.
4. Right to be free from coercion except under due process.
5. Right to equitable treatment and protection under all Authorities.

## Article XVII - Civil and Procedural Rights

1. Right to seek redress before impartial tribunals.
2. Right to transparent enforcement processes.
3. Right to access publicly logged information except privacy-protected data.
4. Right to political participation without discrimination.
5. Freedom of Assembly and Association. Subscribers may form or join peaceful assemblies, associations, and cooperative bodies.
6. Freedom of Movement. Subscribers may move freely within the jurisdiction subject only to narrowly tailored, time-limited safety measures enacted under this Charter.

## Article XVIII - Definitions and Interpretive Rules

### Section 1 - Key Definitions

1. Lead Emergency Authority - The Authority first declaring a qualifying emergency and recognized by the ECO as having functional jurisdiction over the precipitating threat.
2. National Decision - An approval threshold requiring sixty percent (60%) of all Subscribers unless otherwise specified in this Charter.
3. Privacy-Protected Information - Personal data regarding health, identity, communication, finances, location, or other intimate details intrinsic to personal autonomy; may be operationalized by Legislative Acts but not narrowed in ways that infringe Articles XVI or XVII.
4. Qualified Persons - Individuals certified by an Authority-Certification Body (ACB) for competence, impartiality, and ethical reliability within designated service domains.
5. Authority - A governance body formed under Article II with a sealed functional scope.
6. Representative - Any Subscriber receiving delegation tokens from others.

### Section 2 - Authority-Certification Bodies (ACBs)

1. ACBs are independent bodies responsible for certifying Qualified Persons.
2. ACB standards must be publicly transparent, open to examination, and subject to Charter Court review.
3. No ACB may discriminate except on competence and ethical criteria.

### Section 3 - Non-Contradiction Rule

All Authority Charters, Legislative Acts, and subordinate rules must conform to this Charter. Conflicting provisions are void.

## Article XIX - Supremacy, Adoption, and Incorporation

1. This Charter is the supreme governing instrument of all jurisdictions adopting it.
2. Authority Charters, Legislative Acts, and legacy laws are pre-empted to the extent they conflict with this Charter.
3. Any lower-level law in contradiction is void to the extent of the conflict.
4. Jurisdictions adopting this Charter shall incorporate all Authorities operating within their scope.
5. Adoption requires a National Decision.

## Article XX - Transitional Provisions

1. Initial Subscribers are those voluntarily registering at adoption.
2. Initial ACBs are provisional bodies meeting public transparency and open-exam standards, valid for up to three (3) years pending review.
3. Initial Qualified Persons shall be drawn from publicly credentialed professionals until ACB standards are fully operational.
4. Initial Authorities may be formed using simplified majority thresholds for their first Charters only.
5. All transitional procedures expire after the first full consolidation cycle or ten (10) years, whichever occurs first.

END OF THE CHARTER - CHARTER v0.8
